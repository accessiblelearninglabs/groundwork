Eight steps, eight artefacts, one working file

All content is presented in reading order, with no diagram or scripting. Each step asks one question, follows a process developed through practice, and leaves an artefact the next step depends on. The interactive version presents the same eight steps as a selectable sequence.

Step 1 of 8

Diagnose

Is this a learning problem, and for whom?

The process

The gap between what people do and what is needed is examined for its causes before anything is designed: incentives, tools, information, and conditions as well as skill. The population concerned is named specifically, in their setting, because a resource built for an average learner is built for no one. Most performance problems are not knowledge problems, and finding that out first is the largest saving the method makes.

The artefact

A one-page diagnosis: the gap, the population, and what learning can and cannot change.

What it protects

Nothing is built for a problem that learning cannot solve, and nothing is built without naming who it is for.

Sources: references 2, 4 on the Groundwork page.

Step 2 of 8

Scope

What is the smallest resource that would work?

The process

Scope is set by working backward from what a person must be able to do afterwards, and by what working memory can carry at once — not by how much there is to say. What is deliberately left out is written down, because it is the decision most often revisited and the one most often lost.

The artefact

A scope statement, with what is deliberately left out.

What it protects

The resource is short enough to be used and complete enough to be trusted.

Sources: references 5, 6 on the Groundwork page.

Step 3 of 8

Work with experts

What do the people who know this actually know, and how will it be checked?

The process

Experts reliably omit the steps they no longer notice performing. Structured elicitation recovers them. Every claim in the resource is traced to a named source, so that it can be checked by someone other than its author — and so that the resource can be corrected when the field moves.

The artefact

An expert-collaboration plan and a source list.

What it protects

The resource says what is known, attributes it, and can be corrected.

Sources: references 7, 8 on the Groundwork page.

Step 4 of 8

Write

How does it read to the person who needs it?

The process

Register and reading level are judged against the population named in step one. The text is drafted, tested with people from that population, and revised on what they did with it rather than on what they said about it.

The artefact

A drafted resource in plain language, at the level of its population.

What it protects

The resource can be read by the people it is for.

Sources: references 9 on the Groundwork page.

Step 5 of 8

Visual language and production

What does the form add that the words cannot?

The process

Media are chosen for what they carry, not for what is available: words and pictures together where they help, and nothing that competes with them. Accessibility is a property of the first draft, tested against a published standard and with assistive technology, not a review at the end.

The artefact

A production standard: format, media, and accessibility from the first draft.

What it protects

The resource meets the floor of access before it is offered.

Sources: references 3, 10, 16 on the Groundwork page.

Step 6 of 8

Review

Does it meet the standards the field holds in common?

The process

The resource is reviewed against a named checklist, and the review is recorded: what was checked, by whom, and what changed. A review that leaves no record was not a review, and cannot be repeated.

The artefact

A quality-assurance record against a named checklist.

What it protects

Quality is demonstrated, not asserted.

Sources: references 11 on the Groundwork page.

Step 7 of 8

Obligations

What does this resource owe — privacy, ethics, rights, access?

The process

Every resource collects something, asserts something, and is offered to someone. What it owes under privacy law, research ethics, accessibility legislation, and the rights of the people it concerns is written down before release. This step is the practitioner's own; no part of it is delegated.

The artefact

A one-page obligations register.

What it protects

The people the resource concerns are protected by design, not by afterthought.

Sources: references 12, 13, 14 on the Groundwork page.

Step 8 of 8

Keep current

When will this be wrong, and who will know?

The process

A resource is evaluated on whether something changed in how people work, not on whether they finished it. It is designed to be revised when the evidence says so. The update cycle names its sources of change, and the person who holds it next — because most resources are abandoned, not retired.

The artefact

An update cycle, and the case for the resource written for whoever holds it next.

What it protects

The resource stays true after the designer has left.

Sources: references 15 on the Groundwork page.
